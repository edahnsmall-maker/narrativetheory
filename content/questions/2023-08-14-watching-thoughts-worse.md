Being *aware* doesn't mean you have to *believe* all your thoughts, and even if a thought is compelling, remember that your mind loves feeding you distortions. It likes to give you a part of the story, not the full picture. For example, your mind will recall all the mistakes you made and all the ways you're "flawed," everything you're missing that others have. What it will rarely do is recall all the things you did right, all the ways you're excellent and worthy and lovable, and all the ways you're doing just great in life.

With awareness you don't really have to manage this whole process like a flight controller. You don't have to force yourself to think a certain way. If you can experience things AS THEY ARE without freaking out, without buying the story (see above), just letting it be and not be afraid, you'll see that it passes on its own. It takes a bit of faith but not a ton. And you'll see things will kind of settle and even out. The energy of your mind that's feeding you these narratives (what Eckhart would call the Pain-Body) starts to chill and you'll naturally start to think, feel, and see yourself and your situation in a more balanced way.
