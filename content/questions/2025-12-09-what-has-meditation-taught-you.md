**Does the mind ever settle?** Your mind settles and also never settles. It settles from where you start, but it never completely settles.

**Sometimes.** Sometimes it completely settles. Those are those little moments of opening that my teacher would call kensho. Not satori, but kensho.

**Life stuff and meditation aren't separate.** Your life stuff comes up as your mind starts to settle and you get better glimpses into the nature of mind and how things are constructed when certain levers are pulled, and the main lever is your own resistance to what's happening right now. Life stuff and meditation aren't separate. Life realization moments and thinking moments get mixed in. You start to reflect on your life and you look at it differently, with a little bit more patience and tolerance and less violence and less aggression. You stop rejecting yourself. I don't think "processing trauma" is really how trauma works, but old experiences come into a warm light. Sometimes that changes you; sometimes it fades. The shift is perspective. We don't grow by forcing thoughts. We grow by seeing differently: how experience is constructed, and how we construct ourselves in it.

**Meditation practices work in tandem, not against each other.** Concentration on a single focal point and concentration without any focal point, which I would call Zen meditation, are the same thing at the end of the day. They're a continuum. Both matter and integrate.

**Thinking cannot help you.** The big realizations are not thought, and giving up on trying to conceive or attain them is a big part of the path. That's when your mind eases up and perception becomes clear. When I had my big realization, it wasn't because I thought my way there.

**Inquiry is required.** This isn't a static practice. In Rinzai (and Buddhism generally), you're encouraged to have an insight into the nature of self, and that isn't insight as thinking. It's a realization that happens when you are focused and looking really carefully.

**Look carefully and openly.** You have to look carefully and openly, with a really open or a clear mind.

**Teachings connect.** Teachings connect and make sense. Zen teaching is mostly stories, not didactic lessons, but they plant themselves in your head and sprout when the time is right.

**Kindness shows up.** Kindness is woven into all of this. Not the end goal. More the end state.

**Existential pain softens.** Existential questions and the desperation behind them soften when you see through the self thing and look at what's more fundamental, immediate, mysterious, always evaporating, always changing. What are we made of? What is the "we"? Have you ever found it? Bodhidharma said, "Produce your mind and I will pacify it." The student said, "I can't find it." Bodhidharma said, "There, it is pacified."

**Sudden awakening, gradual cultivation.** Openings happen, but you must cultivate them. Master Chinul called it "sudden awakening with gradual cultivation." Once openness appears, you have to work with it.

**There's always a core paradox.** Zen tries to attain non-trying. The point of meditation evaporates. The journey from A to B evaporates. The evaporation is the journey.

**Practical tip.** Stay awake, don't get sucked all over the place with thinking, don't fill your head with ideas. A focal point is a great first step.

**The only thing worthwhile.** I encourage people to practice because it's the only thing worthwhile I've found: being skeptical, open-minded, and willing to look.
