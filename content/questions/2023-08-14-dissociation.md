Dissociation in psychology typically implies a feeling of disconnection from one's experience (body, thinking, reality). Mindfulness or presence or whatever you want to call it is the opposite: you feel more connected and more established in your body and environment. You feel things as more real rather than less. While you stop *identifying* with your thoughts (meaning you don't derive your identity from the *content* of your thinking) you still remain aware of your thinking. In fact you kind of have to be to remain present. Your thinking is a part of your experience like anything else is.

One more note here: when you are not identified with your thinking, the question arises: so what ARE YOU? What is the relationship between your experience and you, if you are not your thoughts? Eckhart discusses this, but I think it's better left to one's own investigation.
