In my experience trying to not think is a recipe for frustration and more importantly, not even necessary. I try to focus on *letting* my relationship to thoughts and everything happening around me go from MANAGING to ALLOWING and HAVING. Stillness is always there when you stop trying to produce an experience and say hello to the experience you have, even if that experience is noisy. Sometimes that attitude can slow thoughts down, but in my personal experience, they are usually still there, just not running the show.

I don't even think Zen Masters really have no thoughts. For one thing, you need thoughts to function in life. For another, your mind is still secreting thoughts even after you have enlightenment experiences. There's some debate about that point in Buddhist literature about Buddha, but I'm certain that if I asked all the Zen Masters in my school about thoughtlessness, they would laugh. Some have, which is why I'm confident in my assertion. What they also say, however, is that they're better at recognizing and putting their thoughts down.
