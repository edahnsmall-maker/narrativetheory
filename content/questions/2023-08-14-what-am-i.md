This is based on my experiences during retreat, but please ask a teacher... The self "you" think "you" are is not actually a thing with a continuous existence. If you look at what makes you up, actually, all you find is experience. And if you look at what that experience is, it's 1) not YOU, it's the sounds "you" hear, the things "you" see, and it's the sensations and thoughts that arise from within "you" and 2) the stuff that's normally thought of as "you" -- typically the thoughts, the body feedback, the "feelings" (though I detest that word because of how vague it is) -- is also all changing. In the midst of that constant flux, there's no way a stable "you" could even survive. It's like trying to build a house on quicksand. It just keeps collapsing. It's the same thing with the idea of a continuous "you." It keeps collapsing, so it can't exist as this separate, continuous entity.

So what does that mean in terms of "your" identity? Who, or what, are you? Well it's gotta be something. But if it isn't this continuous "you," and "you" happen to just be comprised of everything else going on outside of "you," then you must be everything, and everything must be you. There's a difference in making sense of that proposition logically and actually seeing it and internalizing it. But the path is contemplation, introspection, investigation, and observation along the lines of what I mentioned above, hence the question "what...am...I?"
