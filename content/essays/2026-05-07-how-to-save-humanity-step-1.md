One of the 4 great vows Zen students chant is to cut through all delusions.

What does this mean to you?

There are the obvious delusions like everyone's out to get me or aliens are outside my door. (They're actually in the ocean.)

But that's not what this vow is talking about. Delusions are subtle. The fundamental delusion is I-me-my: the delusion that a separate you exists that's the receiver of sensations, perceptions (things), mental formations (thoughts/motivations/emotions), and the driver of the body—the manager inside that calls the shots. Where is that, though? Show it to me.

The I-me-my is a delusion that builds and protects itself. It chases material things. It chases status and fame. It chases gratification. And it fears for itself. And somewhere in that mess, it starts to fight. It creates anger. It creates enemies of people. And it can drive them to madness. It creates a whole world of meaning, drama, betrayal, and loyalties.

And like any good delusion, you're never aware that it's a delusion because it all looks so real.

But how lucky are we, that we get to test those assumptions? We sit quietly and let our minds start to thaw out. The grip of thinking starts to ease, and occasionally let go completely. In those moments, if we look carefully, we see how much of the drama—the delusion—was mind-made. Enemies seem less despicable. Situations seem less hopeless. Possibilities start to blossom. We see how much of our suffering and our predicament was all just delusion. We cut through it for a moment, and then maybe get a sense of ease and lightness, unburdened by the heavy thinking and the volatile projections it generated. Joy arises. Kindness arises. Wisdom arises. Not through force, but through letting go.

And now I finally get to my point: everyone has this potential. We all have this Buddha nature.

Who is your worst enemy when they've cut through all their delusions? Your political rival? The family member you can't stand? Underneath it all, beyond their personal delusions, we're all peaceful, joyous, kind. Remembering that is how we humanize and relate to those we've written off as lost, and how we start the process of repair, even when all hope is lost.
