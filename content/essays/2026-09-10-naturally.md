Where does spiritual practice take us, and does it take us anywhere?

That's a question I ask myself a lot. I think we all ask it every time we sit down. We use it as a guide to start the "process" of meditation. A goal that we have in mind that we work our way towards. But that goal always falls apart sooner than later, and sometimes in dramatic fashion. I've been calling that the collapse of effort.

When effort collapses something else starts to emerge. That something else isn't "born" per se; it doesn't come into existence. Rather, something clears up and reveals what was already there, like clouds scattering to reveal a shining sun.

What is that thing?

Naturalness.

Naturalness just means existing without hindrance. I breathe the way I am breathing. I am silent when nothing is happening. I think when I am thinking. It's not that you're never thinking, but the effort to constantly steer things towards goals we set up is softened. That constant steering is really a form of warfare we engage in with our present circumstances. So naturalness is really a form of inner peace.

It's a peace you can feel and start to become sensitive to. You can see how it operates in the world, how it solves problems, how it looks at things. It's a peace you can trust. And it was always there.
