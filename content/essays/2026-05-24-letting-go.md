I think the hardest thing in Zen is letting go. I actually think that's the only thing that you really need to do.

You get ideas, you have lectures, you read a lot. So you're building your understanding of the world and of the terrain of enlightenment, or of human experience and enlightenment.

But Zen would say that understanding isn't the path. The whole idea is not that understanding gets you to the end faster. The end comes when you learn how to relax and stop thinking. And there are different ways to do that depending on the person's mind.

## Meditation and trying

If you're living in China and you're a monk and you've been practicing Zen for a long time, they might give you something to blow up your whole fucking brain, because they want you to get to the point where you're no longer thinking and trying to get in the way.

That is the hardest thing.

Everybody's always trying to get somewhere, and then they come to meditation and they're still trying to get somewhere. They're trying to get to meditation.

So we have to stop that. If we can really stop that for a while, and we're not trying to get anywhere in meditation, that's when meditation starts. That's real meditation. That's Zen meditation.

## The paradox

That's hard because if you try to do it, you break it.

If you try to meditate and get to letting go, you're not letting go. You're just trying something.

So you have to let yourself be wherever you are and slowly start to come in contact with what's going on in the moment and in your body and yourself, without trying to steer it, without trying to put it in a box, without trying to label it, without trying to do it so you get somewhere.

You can't strategize your way into figuring it out. You figure it out through practice, not through thinking.

## Enlightenment

And if you can manage that, if you can just kind of figure it out through practice, then that is enlightenment.

It's just a different level of enlightenment compared to really deep ones, but still the same experience: don't know.

## Emptiness

If you really want to get fancy with it, you look into this question of self and ask: what exists in me that's real? What's temporary and what's permanent? Is there anything that's really permanent that belongs to me? Or is everything I think belongs to me just made up by everything else?

That's called emptiness.

And when you experience that, even for a few seconds, it can change your whole understanding of things. It's transformative.

But it's transformative because you let go of everything. You haven't gained anything. You've lost.

## No mission

People think you're gaining shit in Zen. You never gain anything. You just stop searching for things.

That's enlightenment to me. When you don't have a mission anymore, and you can really open up.
