Someone apologized to me after meditation last week for fidgeting so much. As a former professional fidgeter, I could relate. When I started this practice 20 years ago, I was basically popping and locking on the meditation cushion for a solid 30 minutes.

I told them it was fine (it really is), but that it actually helps to try and control it a little. The reason is that the body and mind are really one unit.

Don't take my word for it, look at your own experience. When your body is fidgety, your mind is fidgety too. Thoughts pull you left and right and you get lost in 'em. The body and mind are both agitated together.

And when things start to slow down (after a little effort and patience), they slow down together. The breath becomes softer and slower. The mind starts to settle and slow down. It gets better at noticing and keeping focused. And eventually you may find yourself just sitting quietly with no specific agenda.

In the same way we put effort into keeping the mind focused, we can put effort into keeping the body focused, and that simply means not fidgeting so much. It might seem like you're going to explode if you don't obey the fidget, but that feeling eventually goes away. It has to be done with some care and kindness, like all things, but a little effort goes a long way.

Ultimately I think restlessness is just the leftover momentum of the mind and body when we first start to sit. Our minds are still firing on all cylinders, looking to turn inputs into outputs (results). And we apply that to meditation. We're going to do something, achieve something, bend our own untamed nature to the sheer force of our will.

Eventually we get to see that the whole idea of there being something to get to and something to do was just delusion. The defining feature of meditation is the absence of goals and progress towards them. That means that ultimately, the best thing we can really do with our restlessness is…nothing. Don't fight it, don't manage it, don't rush it out, and don't hold onto it. Let it be there.

Let everything be just how it already is.
