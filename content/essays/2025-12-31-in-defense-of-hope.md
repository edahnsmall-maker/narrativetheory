I check the news about 150 times a day and when you do that, it's very easy to get discouraged. We start getting a sense that there's a giant malevolent machine in motion that can't be stopped. Our friends start to express that to us too. The future seems like it's already been gamed, engineered, and decided against us. So what can we do? What's the point? You can feel this way about the world, about your life, about a situation. That perception and feeling hardens over time and seems less like a belief and more like a fact.

But it's not.

We have this Zen practice we do that basically comes down to a single phrase "put it all down." Put it all down means let go of the thought structures and opinions and ideas. Stay open. Even for a minute, let it go and see what's real right now. That's our best chance of engaging a situation wisely and correctly.

That's where I find hope: not in what I know, but in what I don't know. I don't know what will happen next or how things will play out. I can't track every variable and even if I could, there are always surprises. I don't know the future but I know I am a part of it and can do my share as best I can.

Here's the story about a bird that Zen teacher Bobby Rhodes likes to tell:

A little bird sees the forest on fire and starts flying back and forth to a stream, dipping her wings and beak in the water and carrying drops back to the flames. She knows the fire is too big and that it won't work, but she keeps flying anyway. The smoke gets thicker, her wings get heavier, and eventually she can't make it back to the water. She falls into the fire and dies there, having used her whole life doing the one thing she could do.

I don't think that story is sad or morbid. I think it's kind of beautiful.
