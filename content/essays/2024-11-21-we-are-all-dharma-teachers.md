We are all dharma teachers,  
But we don't all believe it.  
We think Dharma is philosophy.  
What kind of philosophy is 3 pounds of flax?  
Or a cypress tree in the garden?¹  
Who needs more ideas!  
I think we have enough.

To glimpse truth for one second is a gift:  
The truth of Me, the truth of change,  
The truth of emptiness and identity.  
"Where can dust collect?"²  
All that stuff.

Why bother chasing things when there's no one to benefit?  
But then what?  
What does it mean  
To stop chasing?  
To relinquish conflict, choose peace,  
And cultivate harmony in this world?

To let this heart lead the way, with courage, wisdom, and boundless patience.  
With hope not despair.  
Speaking words that soothe rather than agitate.  
Spreading cheerfulness, humor, levity, acceptance.  
Helping.  
Listening.  
Caring.  
Letting go.

This is true Dharma.  
There is more to learn from a single act of kindness  
Than a thousand sutras.

This is the Dharma and we are its teachers.

---

¹ A monk asked Dong Sahn "What is Buddha?" Dong Sahn replied, "Three pounds of flax." When another monk once asked Joju "What is Buddha?" he replied, "The cypress tree in the garden."

² Huineng, the Sixth Patriarch of Zen, wrote this verse to challenge Shenxiu's metaphor of polishing the mind like a mirror: "Bodhi originally has no tree, / The mirror also has no stand. / Buddha-nature is always clean and pure; / Where can dust collect?"
