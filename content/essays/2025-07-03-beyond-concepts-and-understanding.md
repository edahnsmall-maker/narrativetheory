Like many people, Alan Watts got me interested in Zen long ago, but it's really Zen that got me interested in Zen. The stories were so bizarre, and somehow, people are getting enlightenment! "How? I gotta understand!" And isn't it ironic, don't you think, that it's precisely this drive to understand things that these koans were trying to target. What does understanding really get you?

When we think we understand something, we've made it into a concept, and concepts are just thinking, abstractions, constructs. They aren't real. Zen is a pursuit of what's real, beyond delusion, beyond concepts, beyond understanding. "Understanding cannot help you!" our Zen Master used to exhort, and it couldn't help the people in these koans either. They tried to make sense of them in the same way we all do, and they eventually failed. But that kind of failure is healthy because it opens the door to what lies outside of our thinking and ideas and understanding.

And what is that that lies outside of ideas and understanding?

The sun is shining and the cars go by.
