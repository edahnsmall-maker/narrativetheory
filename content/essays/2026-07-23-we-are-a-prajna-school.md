I heard this story from Bobby Rhodes.

As a Zen student Bobby once attended a retreat hosted by another school of Zen. They gave their own meditation instructions which was just concentration on the word MU.

The word MU became a thing because of a famous exchange between a student and Zen Master Joju about whether a dog has Buddha nature. 🐶

The backstory to the backstory is that Buddha said all sentient beings have Buddha nature, and if a dog is sentient, he must have Buddha nature, right?

So the student asks Joju if a dog has Buddha nature and Joju responds "MU!" Mu in Chinese means no, nothingness. So on the surface it sounds like Joju is saying "no," but if you read it that literally it doesn't actually make sense. So where does that leave you? Don't know!

So back to the story, at this retreat everyone would just practice MU. MU in the morning, MU in the evening, MU when they're eating, MU when they're walking. MU, MU, MU. By the end of the retreat Bobby said she felt high and energetic.

When she told Zen Master Seung Sahn about it, he said "We are not a samadhi school! We are a prajna school!"

Samadhi is the quality of concentration that arises when we keep the mind focused. Focusing on anything with patience and diligence develops concentration. When the mind is concentrated, it's still, and sometimes that can bring a sense of relief and joy. This is what the retreat was focused on.

But that's not where the path ends.

Our school is a prajna school. Prajna means wisdom and it's one of the six paramitas (virtues) we cultivate through practice. Wisdom is the product of seeing clearly and looking deeply into the fundamental questions of life: what am I? what is this? what is my relationship to this? what is the nature of reality? what does this all mean together?

Your mind will try to apprehend these questions and look for verbal answers. It will try to assemble it all together into some kind of consistent, logical answer that ultimately unlocks the nature of universe and enlightenment in some explosion of joy and light and intelligence. Well, good luck with that.

That's exactly what the student in the MU story was trying to do. He was trying to create a framework and get his mind around something he didn't understand to make it all fluid and elegant—it being his ideas, his philosophy. But Joju saw this coming so he threw a wrench into the student's philosophy: "MU, and good luck with the wrench!"

If we want to truly learn and develop some scintilla of wisdom, we need to put down the philosophies and thinking and analysis and just look. Pay attention. What's happening right now? Before ideas, before thinking, what do you have?

MU!
