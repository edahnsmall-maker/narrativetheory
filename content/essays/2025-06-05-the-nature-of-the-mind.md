We survived another week! 🥳

We don't really celebrate basic survival, though, do we? Not unless we're under threat.

It makes me think about the nature of the mind. The mind doesn't naturally look at what's here; it looks at what's missing. "I need this, I have to do that, I'm missing this in my life, I don't feel as calm as I want, etc, etc, ETCETERA."

We have a fantasy that if we check off all the things, finally, we'll be done. At peace. No longer hungry and searching for the next thing.

But as we check one thing off, a funny thing happens: a new goal always appears further out, and that starts the never-ending cycle of wanting, doing, wanting, doing, never reaching a meaningful conclusion because the game is rigged.

You could say Zen is the practice of examining the game and taking it apart piece by piece. When we practice Zen (meditating, don't-know) and discipline our mind, we see how the mind works, and how we get sucked into the game. We develop the ability to step out of it—out of the constant stream of thinking that's always looking to do something and get somewhere. And we get a chance to look into some of the fundamental assumptions that put the game into motion: me, my reputation, my legacy, a separate entity that's always in conflict with the world.
