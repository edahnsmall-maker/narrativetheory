This week I had the joy of making a semi-emergency visit to the eye doctor. (I'm fine, I promise: it's just sickness old age and death.)

Sometimes it's your eye that needs medicine, and sometimes it's your "I" (the I-me-my ego thing) that needs medicine.

Buddha was kind of an "I doctor". He found himself in this weird position that we all do: struggling, confused, disconnected, searching, anxious, …unsettled. And he decided he was going to figure it all out. His quest eventually led him to examining his own nature and the way the mind operates—creating needs and frictions that feed this idea of me.

But what is this "me" thing? Where is it? What do I find when I look there? What do I mean by "I" look there? It feels like I'm the manager running things from behind my eyes, but how accurate (and how permanent) is this entity?

The I doctor doesn't fix your "I". He/she doesn't strengthen it or demolish it. The I doctor simply tells you where to focus so you can really see things clearly. You can see the truth of your self, realize that there's nothing that ever needed fixing, and there was no one to fix it anyway.
