First off, retreats are hard, but life can be hard, and sitting quietly for 8-12 hours a day for a week to a month won't be the hardest thing you'll ever do. No one said liberation will be easy, but we pursue it because we have an intuition that something funky is going on. Everyone has their own question or thorn, but I think we at some point see that our state of mind has a lot to do with how we experience the world, and that's your first big clue that thinking (generally) might play a bigger role than we anticipated.

The first couple days of a retreat is just your mind settling. Your whole energy changes. You become soft and compassion opens up. You aren't fighting with things. Your thoughts turn positive, sometimes heart-breaking in a good way. You might feel love towards yourself and forgiveness, and you start feeling like your old self.

That starts to deepen and by week 2 the silence settles in. You're no longer having to work to maintain it, it's just there, and you're just there, like a flower. Things are simple. There isn't much you have to do, just wake up, show up, bow, chant, sit, eat, rest, rinse (there ARE showers) and repeat.

Somewhere along the way the teachings you've absorbed over the years appear when you need them. It's like they were leaving clues all over the place and you're on a roll. Things begin to click. And you start having moments of feeling awake, here. Be here now, as Ram Dass exhorted. Thinking takes a back seat. It's still there, but it's in the background. "Aha, so that's non-attachment!" you say.

All the while you're receiving personal guidance from teachers who have walked the path before you. They're feeding you riddles and questions to force you to look at this question of self and identity without resorting to the usual analysis and wordplay. What are you? What is this? What are you looking at, and who's doing all the looking? What do you find? What don't you find? And if you're really lucky maybe you get a little insight into this question. (It's all the same question.) A little insight can go a long way, maybe even helping you really grasp the meaning of the phrase "no attainment with nothing to attain" and put an end to all the searching and malaise.

There's no guarantee that sitting a retreat has any of these moments, but attending, applying yourself, and challenging yourself, invites these experiences to occur, and when they do it can reorganize how you see things.

I think short retreats are like baby long retreats. You go through a similar journey, just on a compressed scale. So…try it.
