There is a strong relationship between framing, koans, leadership, Zen, and locus of normal. We can think of these relationships in sequences.

Starting with Zen: Zen is non-attachment. The mind isn't occupied by thinking and narrative and is instead in a fluid state that is laid back and attentive to surroundings, as well as open to spontaneous reactions (upon which creativity, humor, and keen observation all rely). This mindset is not moved (at least not for more than a moment) by needs and intellection.

The student-practitioner can sink further and further into this attentive, watchful state. We can also call it mere presence or pure presence. These are all just words to describe a word-free state of being.

Zen koans are attempts to test the student to see if they will leave (or be evicted) from this state and enter into a non-Zen thinking state. On the surface, the teacher asks the student a question that tempts them into their thinking. But there's another game happening, and that is: will the student jump out of their frame, their feeling of presence and comfort, to navigate the world through the eyes of the teacher, trying to get the answer correct? They abandon their frame in the presence of an authoritative teacher who has a strong grip on their own frame.

A similar dynamic occurs in the business world, as well as in relationships and other sorts of conflict. A boss talking to a subordinate will compel the subordinate to adopt their frame. The adoption is more likely when (1) the adopter is likely to give up their frame (e.g., they have a strong fear response), (2) the frame disseminator uses threats to encourage frame abandonment and adoption, and (3) the disseminator has a strong frame grip (let's say they practice Zen, or don't have much fear).

The "locus of normal" is a construct that represents the sense that one's experience is normal. It compels others who have a weaker locus of normal to abandon their frame. Celebrities, leaders, and dominant persons have strong ones, as do Zen practitioners and masters. What behaviors are acceptable? What's cool? What's appropriate to say and do at a club? The locus of normal is also a key player in couples' disputes. Whose reality and narrative will control?

A leader and a Zen Master will have the common quality of holding their frame in the face of conflict. The absence of fear and a strong sense of self-esteem will likely be present in both as well, as will spontaneity, humor, and creativity.
