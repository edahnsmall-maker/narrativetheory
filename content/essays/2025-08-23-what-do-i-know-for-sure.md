Okay, this is what Descartes said. He said, I think, therefore I am. Right? But what he should have said is, what do I know for sure? That's what he was trying to get to. What do I know for sure? His fundamental assumption was, I think, therefore I know that I exist. But he phrased it in a weird way. He already made this concept of I. He introduced it into the language because that's how we all experience things.

But what he should have said is, what do I know for sure? Well, I know there's some experience. Because I see something. But I shouldn't say I. Because that's a big assumption. What do I know for sure? Well, I know that there is experience. And it's not me. It's the tree. It's the you. It's the thing. It's the air. It's the sounds. And there's thinking and feeling. It's kind of weird. But all these things are all there is. And there's no separate you to them.

So the whole fucking universe, everything, is making up everything. Everything is making up everything. It's making up its identity. So while we sit here talking, I'm making you up. And you're making me up right now. And the tree is making me up. And there's no separate me. And there was never a connection. In Buddhism this idea is called Indra's net. You have these jewels. They're empty. But they all reflect each other. And they make a beautiful pattern. You see it on the jewels. But it's real. There is never a separate you.

And if you could really, really experience that. Because it's different to talk about it. You can't intellectualize it really. But it's helpful to talk about it a little bit. I'm not a full Zenist. I believe in talking. Zen doesn't believe in explaining. Zen is really about getting you immediately into, shocking you into, the experience of not being in your head. And the route to that is by asking the question of what am I? And really digging into what that is. We just don't talk about it enough. We just talk about it as a question.

The whole point of Zen is to wake you out of that, to wake you out of that mind-dream. But the thing is that nobody knows that they're in the mind-dream until they're out of it, or until they've had a chance to come out, and there's lots of ways to come out of it. You can use meditation as a long-term strategy, and I think it's effective. It helps you see through bullshit because it calms your own thinking down.

Sometimes you just learn things, and you realize, oh, I've been... Okay, tell me this. Does this ever happen to you? You hate a fucking co-worker. You just think they're such fucking pieces of shit, or whatever. And the reason why is because they wronged you in a meeting, or something like that, or they disrespected you. And something happens that just ends that.

What ends that? Sometimes it's a little time and fatigue, and sometimes it's having another good situation happen that brings you together. But sometimes the reason you come together is because you learned that you were wrong about something. You had misinformation. "Oh, I thought you sent that email to Karen. I didn't realize that wasn't you. My bad. I'm sorry." And you realize everything just doesn't look the same way, and it doesn't feel the same way. Because that part of the dream has died, because you see what the truth is. You see that you made it all up, and we're always making shit up, but we don't realize it.
