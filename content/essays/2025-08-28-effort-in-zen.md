What kind of labor does Zen require?

There are so many different intersections between effort and Zen. Striving for enlightenment, wisdom, clarity, meaning…whatever you call it….all require a great effort—one that's sustained, focused, and not always easy. It requires courage, openness, humility, perseverance, and time.

And there's a paradox when it comes to effort too: the more we want to get to a place of peace or clarity or don't-know, the more it eludes us. Striving, clinging, and pursuing goals stirs the mind and stirs up the small-I, the sense of an isolated self that traps us. After all it's desire (tanha) that causes suffering. On the flipside, no effort produces no results at all.

## Buddha's Analogy

In the Sona Sutra, the Buddha offered advice to a monk named Sona who had practiced so hard his feet bled. Buddha explained that effort is like tuning a stringed instrument: when the string is too tight, it snaps. Too loose, it doesn't play at all. In the same way, our effort has to be tuned. Too tight and we cling, divide, suffer. Too loose and we just languish.

When we sit, we start with effort. We carve out time and maybe have an idea of where we want to get to. After focusing the mind and putting down our thinking, we eventually get to a point where we put it all down: our stories, our goals, our resentments, our preconceptions. Maybe it's more accurate to say they drop on their own. Clarity ("don't-know mind") begins to emerge as effort drops away. Our mind becomes a mirror that just reflects and intuits how to respond.

Effortlessness takes effort. Effort takes time.
