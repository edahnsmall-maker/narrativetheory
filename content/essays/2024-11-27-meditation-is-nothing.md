Let me start by saying that I've been meditating for almost 20 years and my mind is still crazy. I cannot stop it usually for more than a few seconds. I'm lucky if I get minutes, and that takes usually a couple hours of meditating. But a few minutes of meditation a day actually weirdly add up.

Most people have difficulty with meditation because they don't understand what it is. In truth… really… are you ready for this? Are you sitting? Sit down. I'm serious. Okay, I'm not serious. I'll tell you. Meditation… is…

Are you sitting?

Meditation is nothing.

Holy shit, are you ok?

I really mean it. It's actually nothing. It's not a thing you can DO because it's not a doing thing. That's what makes it different, but also means it's hard to do, because you can't DO anything. Any kind of doing is going to immediately make a problem because it's just another way of doing SOMEthing. And we're always doing SOME THING.

We're always looking, acting, doing, planning, worrying, wandering. We're always doing something. DOING. SOME THING. Meditation is the practice of not doing anything and watching how not doing anything starts to quiet your mind and dumps you out in the present moment, because that's all that's happening outside of your mind. It's the remainder when your mind starts to slow down. And a few seconds is all you need to really get benefit from it, though slowly. Double-though: significantly and meaningfully.

The first time I really sat to meditate I was fortunate enough to listen to Adyashanti's meditation instruction. You can hear Adyashanti's wisdom in everything he says. It's really insane. And when he talks about meditation, he speaks beautifully. He encourages you to forget about meditation. Don't try to meditate. Forget about your goals and objectives. There wasn't a ton of other instruction.

And that helped. I was quiet for a while. Really! I was shocked. The years of persistent thinking and worrying and wandering and all the bullshit was quiet. And I was relaxed. My body and breathing slowed. I was there. Holy shit I was here!

And then I couldn't get it back. I couldn't redo it. I listened, sat, waiting, nothing.

The fuck.

THE FUCK!

Why is that?

Think about it for a sec.

\* \* \*

Should I just end the chapter here in very Zen form? Nah, let's continue.

You can't recreate anything because you never created anything to begin with, AND the ACT OF CREATION is a DOING, and meditation is a not doing. It's what's left when you stop DOING.

I will confess that after that, meditation has changed. In fact, it constantly changes. It's always evolving. It's good to dump all your expectations.

It changed and also remained a challenge or puzzle that my mind loves. How does one sit to meditate without having a goal? What's the best way to get rid of goals if getting rid of goals is itself a goal? This isn't just a word game, this is a serious dilemma.

I still think. But now I wait. I don't do anything. I just wait. Because I've noticed that whatever I do, the process is always the same. After 15-20 minutes my mind slows and I realize that I never actually do anything. It's a lesson I relearn with every effort. But I learn it.

And that's when everything slows. My mind stops chasing. It stops moving. It stops trying to meditate. It stops having any kind of goal. And I'm just sitting here. Like a sounding board, vibrating with everything that's vibrating me, but nothing else. Not also thinking. Not making a story. Not wondering where I am. And if I do, that just comes and goes too. And eventually the silence and sense of being here (or BEING) just takes over more and more.

When you go on an extended retreat (I've been on many retreats, but about four that lasted two to four weeks of about 12-17 hours of Zen practice a day) you deepen this sense of Being. And your mind starts to really quiet. Your mind kind of sharpens, because for one thing, your focus isn't diffused anymore. You aren't thinking of 17 things at once, including what's happening around you, who you are, self-consciousness, etc. You're just looking at what's here with a really sharp, purified mind.

If you go on this kind of retreat, it's not just your mind that changes, it's your whole outlook and way of experiencing things that changes. And how you relate to yourself, and to others. The way you feel, the way you see the world, your confidence, your outlook, your feeling of closeness vs. anxiety, your ability to see causes, your ability to discern truth, your bravery. It ALL changes.

And then you have the big insights too, about self, reality, "you," how everything fits together in your life to some degree. That's when the really deep change starts to happen.

When you're on retreat you see yourself with patience and love. Your view of the world starts to change. You become fragile, almost heartbroken. You find love, and sometimes not love that you hope turns to love. You can confront your fears.

And through that process, or maybe alongside, because the truth is I'm not sure about the causation, your whole experience of life changes. And that shows you very immediately how much of our experience is driven by our mood and our mind in that mood.

And that becomes the most important lesson in your life. Because it shows you the beginning of the map. The map tells you where to go, and you found a piece of it. It's not that I'm not living in reality. I'm living in an AI-enhanced (your mind) version of it that's actually not that great. It's great at making sure you survive. Not great at making harmony, peace, wisdom. That's what will matter to you when you die, so you better start working on it now.
