I think the point of therapy is really to have transformational breaks. And what I mean by break is an interruption in one's cognitive style and the sort of mode they're in, the ambient mode that they're in.

Modes really do stack, or styles of thinking can at least stack. I don't know what we want to call it. Mode or whatever, but they really do stack, and people can be doing all these different things and have this kind of all-pervasive anxiety behind it all.

And then that could affect all the different areas of their life: their relationships, their career, their family, their romance. I feel it myself. And there are these little windows where you get these little transformational breaks. Same thing as meditation. You have these little transformational breaks where you're not in the mind world anymore.

You're not acting as the actor in the mind world.

You are the sort of... I guess you could say you're the silence behind it, the wisdom, the kind of wise silence, the calm silence behind it.

But I don't even know if you could say "you," because that gets very tricky all of a sudden, because then you start getting into these ideas of self and non-self. Really, it's functioning from that before-thinking state, and interacting with life that way, and trusting that that's okay.

And usually the trust kind of comes from that.

It's really interesting, because part of the experience itself is trust, because there's an okayness that is already sort of present in the background, or at least is revealed when the anxiety is lessened.

And the anxiety is just all that thinking and energy and the attachment to it, if you could say. But I really feel like a better word right now is identification with it.

You get absorbed in it.

It encompasses you. So the point of therapy is to engender these moments of dropping the whole façade, and revealing and cultivating that quiet self. And getting comfortable in that. I think it's really getting comfortable in those shoes, where you're not driven by the mind stuff and all of the fears and nonsense that create the whole world of maya, illusion.

So then the techniques for doing that: you could be really creative. You can frustrate, you can ask questions. You can have people reflect on what it might look like to be done with that, as a way to almost pretend, taking it off. Taking off that false self, the persona, the thinking mind, the thinking personality.

You could do experiments.

You could have people reflect on what it means to be that quiet self, when they've felt that, when they don't feel that. Have them get good at identifying their own states: am I functioning from silence, or am I functioning from noise and anxiety and franticness and desperation, as I used to call it. I love that word.

It's a very good word.

So that's really the point, I think, of therapy.

Now, you kind of got to think about, does this cover everything?

I guess, does it cover grief?

I always think about that.

And I don't know if it necessarily covers grief, but maybe it can cover grief.

And maybe the general idea is that people know how to live life and solve their own problems, and the more they can get in touch with that calm root, the better they're going to be able to solve their own problems and just live, and not be suffering all the time.

Maybe that's just the point of life.
