Today we're going to nerd out together on the paramitas. In classic Buddhism, the paramitas are 5 virtues that, when combined with wisdom, lead to liberation.

The 5 Paramitas (Perfections)

1. Dāna — generosity
2. Śīla — ethical conduct
3. Kṣānti — patience
4. Vīrya — energy / effort
5. Dhyāna — meditation / concentration

Today we're going to focus on #3 patience. Before we start, a few questions to ponder:

1. What is patience? What is a lack of patience?
2. Where does patience show up in your life? How does it change things?
3. Why do you think patience was given so much importance in Buddhism?

Let's define patience loosely as the ability to tolerate not getting what you want.

## Outer Patience

In our daily life (work, relationships) you probably already know what patience does: it prevents us from losing our shit all day at the myriad annoyances we encounter. Rather than getting upset and saying something out of anger, and dealing with the karmic consequences of that anger, patience helps us delay, listen, consider, and then choose how we want to best respond. In therapy speak patience is the difference between reacting and responding. The Buddha discussed patience as the antidote to anger/aversion.

## Inner Patience

Patience also comes up in our Zen practice. In fact, I think it's the most important quality we can cultivate.

When we're unhappy with our progress, doubting our ability to meditate, or yearning for immediate results, patience helps us put it all down. Patience helps us let go of our desires (tanha, the thing responsible for suffering), including the desire to experience something other than what we're ALREADY experiencing. When we do that, we come into contact with how things are, what Zen Master Seung Sahn would call "like this." The engine that powers thinking starts to lose steam and clarity begins to grow. Then correct function appears spontaneously, what ZMSS would call "just like this." So when hungry, eat. When tired, sleep. When someone needs help, help them.

These two aspects of patience (daily life & meditation) aren't truly separate, though. They're only separate if you sequester those parts of your life from each other. The longer we practice, the more they start to bleed into one another. If we're conscious about how our mind is operating when we're out in the world, then our entire life can be an expression of Zen.

But that takes time. And patience.
