This one's going to start with a personal share.

When you think about a person on a meditation retreat, you probably picture something serene, peaceful, butterflies landing on your palms, etc. And while some of that can be true, you also have to deal with your mind the same as always, but with a few tweaks. One, it's easier to calm down. Two, it's much easier to get activated. And three, because you're training your mind to stay put, it's easier to see when and how it becomes activated.

Some mind activation is no big deal. A random thought here and there can just be let go and ignored. But other times the thinking is more serious.

I had 2 reactions of the more serious type on retreat, in just 7 days. Both overtook my mind in different ways, changing the way I saw things, felt about them, and engaged (or planned to engage) with them. Neither of these reactions were new though. They're the same type of reactions I've been dealing with my whole life. They're dreams that I get sucked into.

We all have these idiosyncratic dreams. For some people the dream is about rejection ("people hate me, I'm just waiting to confirm it"). Others might slip into anger dreams, just waiting for a target to vent at. Others might dream that the world and they themselves are worthless. And others might dream that everything is dangerous or quickly falling apart. I'm convinced that one day we'll reclassify all the psychological disorders and find new ones by thinking about disorders this way.

So what do we actually do with these dreams?

"Put it all down" is a great answer, but maybe not always practical. Like sleeping dreams, we rarely know when we're dreaming. Our mind convinces us that our perception is accurate and real and fortifies it with facts and elaborate narratives.

This is where dream analysis (or an analogue of it) can come in handy. Not Freudian dream analysis, but just understanding and managing your own personal dream so it doesn't run your life.

- First understand your dream: What personal and relational patterns do I find myself in? When do I escalate and then deescalate, and about what? How does my dream take over? How does it operate in my life? What has it created and caused?
- Then look into it: What's at the bottom of this thing? What's keeping this in motion, specifically? Maybe you get an answer, maybe not.
- Then manage it: How can I keep tabs on it? What can I do when the dream is vivid and active? How do I make things correct when my dream has made a mess? How do I work with it without hating it?

We all have thinking dreams. The second great vow brings that reality to the surface: "delusions are empty, we vow to cut through them all." The question is whether we can wake up from them and restore our status as Buddhas.
