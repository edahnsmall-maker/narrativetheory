Lately I've been reflecting on conflict.

Tensions are obviously rising in the world today. In the past we could turn to leaders to try and soothe those tensions and encourage calm, restraint, and respect, but now we see the opposite: inciting more reactions, outrage, and retribution. Every tragedy becomes an opportunity to pile on more hatred and disgust, which eventually leads to dehumanization and large-scale atrocities.

Where does conflict come from? Conflict exists in a complex ecosystem with incentives and structures that encourage it (social media, politics, wealth gaps) but there's a closer, more intimate origin too: conflict originates in our thinking and how we meet each moment. When our likes, dislikes, goals, and judgments are all active, we meet our experience with conflict. We say we don't like it, we judge it, and we go to war with it with a private strategy to change it. It can show up when we're talking to family, a coworker, looking in the mirror, or even trying to meditate, getting upset with what appears.

Zen is just practicing peace, starting with inner peace. During practice, we suspend (or maybe examine) that endless striving that gives birth to conflict. We don't force it - that's conflict coming through the backdoor - we invite it and stay open. What if I don't have to get something? What if I don't have to get somewhere right now? What if I just sit here and listen? Ah, there's the bird chirping.

When the inner fire is extinguished, the peace and wisdom we always have appears. The conflicts and resentment don't disappear, but maybe hope and possibility arise. Things don't seem doomed and we find ways to respond to our situation creatively, from wisdom, rather than following our habit-mind that thrives on conflict, resentment, and its own self-importance. Maybe then we can do some good and fulfill our vow of saving all beings.
