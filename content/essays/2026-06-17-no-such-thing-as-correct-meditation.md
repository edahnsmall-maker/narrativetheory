The idea that meditation can be good or bad is a little absurd. Because meditation is what happens when the mind starts to chill out a little. And as that happens, the ideas of good and bad, correct and incorrect, are exposed for what they are: just ideas, not actual things. They're fabricated and hallucinated by thinking.

Thinking's job is to help us achieve goals and it does that by comparing, evaluating, judging, and creating tension when things aren't working the way we want. To have a good date [the goal], I need to look nice, but I don't look nice right now [evaluation, judgement], so I need to get a haircut and I need it now [tension, urgency]. (I actually do.)

When our thinking meets our meditation practice—especially during the first few minutes of sitting—it does the same thing: it looks at what's here and tries to decide if it's good or bad. How does it compare to a goal of what meditation SHOULD look like? Am I breathing right? Is my attention right? Is my mind settled enough? Is it supposed to feel like this?

It looks, it compares, it makes a judgment (you're doing good/bad) and maybe stirs up an emotional response (pride/frustration). I'm not suggesting this is good or bad. It's just what happens. It's thinking doing its thing.

If you wait a little bit though, and just focus on something other than thinking (including how good or bad you're doing) something starts to happen on its own. The thoughts slow down a little. Your breathing starts to stabilize. Your body relaxes a little. And the whole idea of goals in meditation starts to seem shaky. What was I after, exactly? Why am I trying to force this? Why do I force anything?

And that's when good, bad, correct, incorrect, like, dislike, all start to fall apart too, because there's no longer something to measure them against. There's no longer a goal or model to follow. I'm just here, and that's enough for now.
