This is a small note for the meditation course, and for meditation instruction in general, or meditation tips. The most important thing in meditation is really your attitude. And it's your attitude when you see your thinking.

You don't have to blot out thinking.

In fact, the Heart Sutra says the bodhisattva depends on prajna paramita, and the mind is no hindrance.

It doesn't say that the mind doesn't exist.

It just says that the mind is no hindrance.

And if I look at some of my own experiences, in fact all of my experiences that have been the most clear have also had a ton of thought, but the thought wasn't the centerpiece. Not only was it not guiding the show; it was happening, but I wasn't in it.

And it's hard to describe the sensation of being in thought and sort of animating it, versus not being in it and letting it drift into the background.

But that is the experience of non-attachment.

And if you react to your thoughts, that's not really non-attachment.

Non-attachment really just means you don't even have to mess with it, because when you start messing with it, that's steering, and now you've got a whole other thing on your mind, which is the content of your experience and the success or failure of your meditation practice.

That is a very important point: meditation practice and the success of it can't be something that you're constantly measuring, because then you're just measuring.

And then guess what?

Your meditation practice sucks.

But really, the attitude that you have when you have those thoughts is just the most important point.

And what is that attitude?

Well, it's kind of easiness, not reacting, not taking the bait, letting it be there, and also realizing that that's not a failure, actually.

It's not a failure at all to have thoughts.

And they come and they go, and what you're cultivating is the background with which they come and go.

You're cultivating the sky, and cultivating the sky doesn't actually require anything proactive.

You don't have to actively cultivate it, but you're kind of developing it at the same time.

So there's maybe no great way to describe the effort there, because it is effort and it's also non-effort, because it's already there by default. Letting the background come more into the foreground is a funny way to put it, but it's not a bad way: letting the background of experience become more pervasive and more of a main character. It's like paying a little bit more attention to the space, or the set, than the actual characters on the set.

The characters come and go in the play, but the set is always the same, or the stage is always the same.

So paying attention to the stage is not a super spiritual thing.

It's just the background for things.

And the background is quiet and it's simple and it's whatever is happening right now. That is the background.

The thoughts are just little actors that come in. So thoughts come and go, and you let them go.

Don't keep your attention on anything specific.

That's the way that I would say it.

And the thoughts come and go. You don't push them. They're in awareness, because how could they not be in awareness?

But then they kind of leave awareness.
