Halloween got me thinking about costumes and masks.

Carl Jung used the term "persona" to describe the social mask we wear to interact with the world. The word comes from the masks worn by actors in ancient theater. For Jung, the persona is the identity we present to others.

But we aren't just presenting identities to others—we also present an identity to ourselves.

We think of ourselves as things—egos—entities inhabiting bodies that make decisions, think, and plan, and have histories and stories attached. But when we look for this ego entity, we never see it. We can't touch it.

When our thinking and emotions are stirred up, this ego-entity becomes more salient and we get lost in the stories and drama that it spins.

When we stop reacting to our thinking and emotions and let them be without getting lost in them, they begin to lose control over our perception. The narrative in our head can completely dry up. That gives us an opportunity to look at things fresh and examine carefully:

What is that narrative? Is it me, or just a mask I wear? Who is the I who wears a mask? How real is that "I"? How persistent? If everything is always in flux, how could there be something permanent that I call myself? And if I can't put my finger on what I am, what's left?

These questions aren't meant to be settled with a phrase "don't-know". They have to be explored deeply and personally with a "don't-know" mind—a mind that's clear, sharp, collected. Theories won't help. Understanding won't help. Even the wisdom of 1,000 Buddhas won't help. They must be explored on your own so they become your own wisdom.

What will you find when you take off your mask?
