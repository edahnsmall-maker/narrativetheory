There's this famous line from Allen Iverson, a guard for the 76ers with a killer crossover (he's my favorite basketball player to watch). He got in trouble for missing some practice sessions and the reporters, never ones to miss an opportunity for scandal, grilled him on it. To which he kept repeating the line "we talkin about practice?!" around 22 times in disbelief and contempt.

Well, today, we're talking about practice.

When someone asks you about your religious background, sooner or later you'll tell them "I practice Zen."

To which most of them will say "you what?"

Before I start yammering, take a second and think about that: what does it mean to "practice Zen"? Is it a philosophy? A set of principles? Vows? Is practicing Zen about action? Or is it just meditating and chanting something in Sino-Korean once a week?

In my own experience, the meaning of "practicing Zen" has changed somewhat over the years.

At first when I started practicing Zen, I was proud to practice it. I thought it was interesting and it made me kind of interesting too by extension. It wasn't all superficial, but that was a part of the appeal. And honestly, I'm kinda glad, because I stuck with it. In practice I was doing the same stuff I do now, but the meaning behind it was different.

As I started getting deeper into Zen practice, it started to become a way of trying to see the world and make sense of it. Everything was viewed through a certain Zenny lens and to be honest, it had a judgmental flavor to it. I was looking for a way to make sense of things and bring order to my life, having all but given up on mainstream religion and the way it dissected the world. Zen provided some order and security amidst confusion and instability.

And then, as I've kept practicing, it's become something different entirely. Not a philosophy or an identity. Not a way to get an advantage. It's a way to get out of that game entirely. An opportunity to un-load (more like software than cargo) the thinking programs that create conflict with others, with myself, with this moment. A way to sense harmony and develop a sensitivity to it.

Practice isn't about getting, it's about pausing and seeing that we're already complete. Not in a grand metaphysical mind-blowing nirvana supernova, but quietly touching the kindness, and love, and wisdom (it's all one thing) that's behind every experience and watching that one thing devour the delusions responsible for that pervasive hum of misery and angst.

That peace and harmony we feel (slightly sometimes, and profoundly at others) just wants to be shared. It becomes a guide for our actions and decisions that supersedes philosophies or ideologies and even causes. It becomes something we can trust in. Why? Because it's obviously trustworthy. There's no better answer than that.

So right now, what does practice mean to you?
