Over the course of meditating (…and struggling (…and learning)) over the last 20ish years, I've noticed myself go through phases where I come up with little mantras, or maybe "themes" is a better word. It's not something I repeat over and over, but something that I say to myself that helps cut through confusion.

In Zen we have a lot of questions that we ask. What is Buddha? What is Zen? What is Mind? What am I? I love that part of the practice because it sparks curiosity and openness.

A question I started asking myself a few years ago is "WHO SAYS?" as in "who says there's something I'm missing?"

So often when I sit to meditate I have an idea of what I should be DOING. Sorting my mind out, sorting my breathing out, becoming clear…I know because I've experienced that before, and I want to return to it.

But that only leads to more mind movement. More agitation. More criticism. More rejection of what's already happening. The assumption is that something is MISSING from my experience. This moment is lacking something. It's incomplete. I am incomplete.

And that's when this "mantra" shows up. WHO SAYS? Who says I'm missing something? Who says this is wrong? And in a deeper sense, who says that I'm lacking in some respect?

I ask myself that question and then just see where it leaves me. There's no intention for anything else to change or improve. Quite the opposite, I sit with whatever confusion or frustration or dissatisfaction I might be experiencing. But now I'm experiencing it 100%, not resisting it anymore. And usually, things begin to clear up, like the Sun coming out after a rainstorm. The whole notion of getting somewhere becomes less important, and sometimes even absurd. What is there to get? What was really missing? Nothing. It was just a delusion. There's nothing to attain. That's truth.

Maybe these words resonate with you, but more important is that we all have this inner wisdom that can guide us in our own way, with our own language, hitting our own mind.

So what is your inner wisdom telling you?
