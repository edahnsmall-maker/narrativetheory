I always liked this saying, maybe because I value truth and knowledge. And freedom. But how does the truth actually set you free?

Maybe it depends on what we mean by truth.

On the simplest level, when we're living with a secret, that secret can turn into a prison you're constantly forced to manage, both externally and internally. Once the truth comes out, it can feel freeing.

But on a more fundamental (and Buddhisty) level, if the truth is what sets us free, it's delusion and ignorance that keeps us trapped. In the Buddha's core teaching, the 12 links of dependent origination, it's ignorance that gets the whole process of samsara going—ignorance of the true nature of reality.

The true nature of reality is not something you casually put into words, because doing so betrays what it is. Truth isn't something you tag with a label and apprehend with your mind like a concept or a fact. Things apprehended by the mind are only byproducts of the mind, not the things themselves.

The truth has a quality that can't be adequately captured in words: the sound of a waterfall, or a rock hitting bamboo, or the single cry of winter geese. Sound has a way of defying linguistic categorization. It's one meaning behind the name of my school, "Kwan Um," which means "perceive world sound." (The other meaning, "perceive world suffering" is equally important.)

Perceiving truth frees us because it requires a still mind. A still mind can't trap us in delusion. It can't tell us what we're missing. It can't tell us what to chase. It can't try to define what we are. A still mind is able to inquire about its own nature and see that not only was there nothing to chase, but there was no one to really chase it.
