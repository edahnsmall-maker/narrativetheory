If you get deep enough into any spiritual or even psychotherapeutic discipline, you realize the importance of acceptance. Acceptance is really the opposite of craving—that thing that's responsible for all of suffering according to the Buddha. The crave is to want things to be different in some way. To accept is to be okay with how things are.

There are plenty of things in our lives that yearn for acceptance:

Accepting loss.

Accepting a situation.

Accepting one's limits and flaws.

Accepting this moment.

I used to fantasize about what it would look like if I was fully accepting of everything. I'd picture myself, fully at ease, finally able to function optimally.

And then I would start to think about how to get there. How do I move into that space? How do I accept something? What does that mean to accept? Can I just will it?

In short: what's the process for accepting something?

One thing meditation has taught me is that acceptance is not a process at all. It's not an event that we undergo. It's not something we can make happen because it's not really a thing at all. Acceptance is a lack-of-thing.

When we make acceptance into a process, we're really just practicing craving. It's as if we're saying, "the pre-acceptance state is inferior. I want that post-acceptance state." A process for getting somewhere implies that there's somewhere better to get to. That's the opposite of accepting things, which is why trying to manufacture acceptance never ever works.

True acceptance is messy. With true acceptance we give up trying to get somewhere else entirely. We stop trying to change things. We stop trying to escape things, avoid them, control them. We stop trying to make sense of them in a certain way and overcome them. We just sit with how they are to the best of our ability, with dignity and composure. If we are confused, we are confused. If we're restless, we're restless. If we're happy, we're happy. We suspend our personal campaigns to change things and get somewhere.

And with a little time and patience, we start to feel restored and balanced again, and the idea that we were ever missing something just seems absurd.
