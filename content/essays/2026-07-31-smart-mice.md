This weekend I was watching an ad (my favorite thing to not do) for facebook which ended with the tagline "Find what you're looking for."

After suppressing my gag reflex, I reflected on what the ad was doing and what we in Zen are doing.

The ad was playing on human desires. "What you want, we'll provide, because we have it, and getting it is going to make your life better." I mean they didn't say that word for word, but it's implied. #truthinadvertising

In Zen we don't crush our desires. But maybe we suspend them to see how they really affect us. We take all our goals that activate this ongoing thinking narrative, and we put it all down. All our needs, dreams, thoughts, goals, even the goals we had for our meditation…all of it gets put down. At first just for a few moments as we focus on something simple besides our thoughts.

A few moments turn into a few seconds, even if it's not pure. Then, with diligence and patience, we start to get a taste of what it's like to just sit, without a goal or an intention or even a reason—don't know. The thinking machine (the desire machine) comes to a halt and we find that there's peace already there. We didn't have to produce it or engineer it. It wasn't something we got; it was something we found we already had.

And that's when we get a life lesson about the relationship between desire, goals, thinking, and peace. Chasing what we're seeking can produce little highs but more often it produces little lows. It keeps us locked into a cycle, like a mouse running in a wheel believing that he'll find relief if he runs fast enough.

But a smart mouse stops running and sees what happens. He realizes that if he doesn't start the process of chasing, he's already good and he's free to spend his time as he pleases.

Be the smart mouse.
