The school of Zen I practice in is named the Kwan Um School of Zen. The term derives from "Kwan Seum," as in "Kwan Seum Bosal," which means "perceive world sound."

What on Earth does that mean? In the Avalokiteshvara tradition, "world sound" points especially to the cries of suffering beings. So, putting it all together, Kwan Um is about perceiving the suffering and problems of the world and responding to them clearly and correctly. The emphasis here is both on the perception and the response.

In Zen, to "perceive" means to experience something without separation. Separation happens when I add my thinking, my identity, my worldview, my beliefs and ideologies, my concerns, my opinions (are you seeing a pattern?). When I generate an identity and conceptual world of good and bad, I create separation between me and the world's suffering.

For example, maybe I don't "count" someone's suffering because I've come to hate or resent that person or group. Or maybe I use their suffering to further my own opinions and agenda. Or maybe I intellectualize it and complicate my understanding and response. Those examples were easy to come up with because human nature is to slip into these habitual patterns.

In Zen we really just practice one thing over and over: disengaging from the ongoing stream of thinking and returning to whatever's happening. At first that might be messy. It can cause turbulence in the form of confusion and even physiological reactions. But after some persistence, the thinking world starts to become more transparent and the power it has over our perception starts to slip. Then we might notice things around us as they come into focus: the floor is brown, the walls are white, and the people around us are intensely suffering right now.
