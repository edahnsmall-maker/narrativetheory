There is a story in Zen circles about a man and a horse. The horse is galloping quickly, and it appears that the man on the horse is going somewhere important. Another man, standing alongside the road, shouts, "Where are you going?" and the first man replies, "I don't know! Ask the horse!"

The horse is your habitual mind. If you don't learn how to get control of your mind, it'll lead you wherever it wants to go: towards delusion, anger, conflict, endless planning, and general dissatisfaction. When you put down all your thinking, you let your true nature guide you towards peace, compassion, and helping others. In my school we use the terms "correct situation, relationship, and function." That just means that your action (your function) is driven by your clear perception rather than a distorted, egocentric perception.

Each moment presents us with a choice: let the horse drag us wherever it wants or calmly get off it and go in a direction that really makes sense.
