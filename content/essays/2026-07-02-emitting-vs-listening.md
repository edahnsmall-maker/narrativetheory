Zen has taught me that there are only really 2 states or modes of being: emitting and listening.

Emitting is a state of production. We're producing thoughts, ideas, plans, and trying to steer things in the direction we think they're supposed to go. When we're thinking, we activate an entire worldview: a complete set of good and bad evaluations that we project outward (and inward). He's bad, they're good, I'm decent at times. They're thinking about me. This is going terribly, etc., etc. This is why in Zen we say "the whole world is made by mind alone."

When we're lost in thought, we get separated (though ultimately we're never separated) from our innate wisdom, kindness, and lightness. At extremes, Emitting can look like psychosis and obsession. You can easily see this if you work in mental health, but there are less obvious forms too: people who are obsessed with their self image, or getting money, or people who live with paranoia or constant friction.

If Emitting is a state of production and putting things into the world, Listening is its opposite. In Listening states, we take in. We observe and absorb. Rather than thinking, we notice with openness. When we're Listening, we aren't deluded by all the mind's ideas and projections and stories. That frees us to express our innate softness and kindness. We become available to help others or just to sit quietly.

Some situations call for Emitting, some for Listening. If you're about to give a dharma talk, it might be strange if you said nothing (although frankly this is the story of how Zen started). The important thing is to not get too lost in Emitting, or to notice when you are and learn how to come back. Notice your thinking. Notice how it hijacks your body and experience. Notice how it covers up the Listening portion. Then you're already back to Listening.
