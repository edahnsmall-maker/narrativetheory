My father passed away two weeks ago. I'd like to share a little of what the experience was like because it's relevant to Zen.

My youngest brother called me to say my father was being taken to the hospital because he had what we thought was a stroke. He was calm but started crying out of fear that something really bad happened. My family gathered at the hospital when we found out that my father passed away at the hospital while getting a CT scan.

We all broke down immediately and began sobbing in a small room called the Consultation Room. He wasn't sick and we didn't have a chance to say bye, but that's life. That's nature. Part of nature is also love and the eventuality of loss.

Alongside the sharp sadness appeared the question of "function". What do I do right now? What's correct? What does my intuition say? It said to help. It didn't say "go help" and it wasn't a thought-process. It was just obvious in the moment.

It was also obvious to provide comfort, to arrange for the next-day funeral, and to let the moments of sadness, laughter, and heartbreak come and go, rise and fall.

The next week was just action and being here. What do I do? How do I help? Not a lot of thinking, not trying to do the "Zen thing," just responding.

It made me reflect about how we teach Zen as a mind-discipline when an equally good way of teaching it is just trying to help with an open mind. Staying open, here, watching, learning, responding as best you can with an open heart. Zen teachers call it "correct function," "helping this world," "saving all beings," but it's easier to just think about it as being helpful. Maybe the years of Zen practice, meditation, retreats, challenging yourself to really attain the teachings helps you locate the role faster, but it's always there, shining brightly.

There's so much wisdom in Zen, at every corner, in every teacher and teaching, every question, every slap of the floor, every hit of the bell, every moment of silence. They're opportunities to wake up from the mind's dream, to look inside, to look at this question of self and identity, and ultimately to figure out how to best function in this world, not "for me," but "for you." That's why we're all here.
